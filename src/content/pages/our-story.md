## From a Single Acre

In the autumn of 1989, Thomas Renard planted his first acre of Cabernet Sauvignon on a steep, rocky slope above the valley floor. There was no grand plan — only a conviction that this hillside, with its volcanic soils and afternoon shade, had something singular to offer.

That first vintage, 1992, produced 90 cases. Thomas gave most of it away to friends and neighbours. He kept six bottles for himself, and when he opened the last one in 2004, he wept.

## Growing Slowly

Maison grew slowly by design. Thomas and Catherine added blocks one at a time — only when they found land that met their standards, and only when they had the time to farm it properly. By 2005, the estate comprised 18 acres. Today it is 42, spread across five distinct blocks, each farmed as its own entity.

The wines are made by hand. The cellar has no automation beyond temperature control. Everything else — sorting, punching down, racking, topping — is done by the same small team that has worked here for years.

## Today

Thomas retired in 2015. Catherine still walks the vineyard every morning. Their daughter Isabelle, who studied viticulture in Dijon, oversees all farming decisions alongside our head viticulturist. Winemaker Elena Marchand makes the wine.

The estate is theirs. The wine is yours.
